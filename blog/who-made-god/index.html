Who Made God?

The question sounds simple, but it helps us see the difference between the Creator and everything He made.

Published 27 July 2026 · Updated 27 July 2026

Practise in the appBack to blog
Answer in brief

No one made God. Everything that begins to exist needs a cause, but God never began to exist. Christianity describes God as eternal, uncreated and self-existent. If God had been made by something else, that maker would be greater and would be the true God instead.

Christians do not believe God is one more object inside the universe. God is the reason there is a universe at all. So asking “Who made God?” can be useful, but it assumes God belongs to the same class as created things. He does not.

Does everything need a cause?

No. The Christian claim is more precise: everything that begins to exist needs a cause. A chair, a star and a human life all begin. God does not. Psalm 90:2 says, “From everlasting to everlasting you are God.” God has no first moment.

This is why the answer is not a special exception invented to protect belief. An eternal God is part of what Christians mean by God. Exodus 3:14 records the divine name, “I AM WHO I AM.” God does not receive existence from another. He simply is.

Why not say the universe is eternal instead?

That is a fair question, but it moves the discussion rather than ending it. The universe is made of changing, dependent things. Its parts do not explain why the whole exists. Even an endless chain of dependent causes would still leave us asking why that chain exists at all.

Christianity points to a necessary Creator who does not depend on anything else. Romans 11:36 says, “From him and through him and to him are all things.” God is not the last link in a chain. He is the source of the whole chain.

Is this just avoiding the question?

It would be avoidance to say, “Everything needs a maker, except God.” But that is not the argument. The claim is that created and changing things need an explanation beyond themselves. God is eternal and uncreated, so He is not the kind of being that needs to be made.

We should also admit that eternity is hard to picture. Our minds are used to beginnings, clocks and causes. Not being able to imagine eternal existence fully does not make it contradictory. It shows the limits of creatures thinking about their Creator.

How to say it gently

You could say: “Christians do not believe everything has a cause. We believe everything that begins to exist has a cause. God did not begin. If He had a maker, that maker would be God. The Christian claim is that the eternal God is the source of everything else.”

What not to say

Do not mock the question or call it foolish. Many people ask it sincerely, and some Christians have asked it too. Avoid pretending eternity is easy to understand. Give the clear answer, then ask what the person thinks could finally explain why anything exists.

A useful next step is to read Psalm 90:1–2 and Exodus 3:13–15 together. Notice that the Bible does not begin by explaining where God came from. It presents Him as the eternal One from whom creation comes.
