If Jesus Is God, Why Didn’t He Know the Hour?

Mark 13:32 makes sense when we take both Jesus’ full deity and His real humanity seriously.

Published 7 August 2026 · Updated 7 August 2026

Practise in the app Back to blog
Answer in brief

Mark 13:32 does not deny Jesus’ deity. The eternal Son truly became human, and in His earthly mission He accepted real human limitations, including growth in knowledge. Christian theology therefore reads the verse through Christ’s two natures: fully God and fully man, one person, without turning His humanity into an illusion.

If Jesus is God, why didn’t He know the hour in Mark 13:32? Christians shouldn’t dodge the wording. Jesus says that no one knows the day or hour, “not even the Son, but only the Father”. The verse is difficult, but it fits the incarnation rather than disproving it.

Does Mark 13:32 say Jesus really did not know?

Yes, the ordinary meaning of the Greek verb is “knows”. Some Christians have suggested that Jesus meant He would not disclose the hour, but that shouldn’t be treated as though “knows” were a mistranslation. The Answerable source card itself makes that distinction carefully. A stronger answer starts by accepting what the verse actually says.

Jesus also grew in wisdom in Luke 2:52. He became tired, hungry and sorrowful. None of this means the eternal Son stopped being God. It means He became truly human. A humanity that never experienced human limits would not be our humanity.

How can Jesus be omniscient and still have limited human knowledge?

The Christian answer is the incarnation. Jesus Christ is one person with two distinct natures, divine and human. His divine nature does not lose omniscience. His human nature remains genuinely human, including a human mind that can learn and know things in a human way.

This is classic Christian teaching, not a modern escape route. The Westminster Confession of Faith 8.2 says the eternal Son took a true human nature, with the two natures joined in one person “without conversion, composition, or confusion”. Does this make Jesus less than God?

No. The mistake is to assume that becoming human required the Son to stop being divine. Scripture says the opposite. Colossians 2:9 says that in Christ “the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily”. Philippians 2:6–8 describes the divine Son humbling Himself by taking the form of a servant.

That also helps us read Mark 13 in context. Jesus’ point is not to invite date-setting. It is to call His disciples to watchfulness because the timing is not theirs to control. The practical force of the passage is humility and readiness, not speculation.

How to say it gently

You could say: “Mark 13:32 really does say the Son did not know the hour. Christians believe the eternal Son became fully human without ceasing to be God. So Jesus could experience real human limits in His earthly life while His divine nature remained fully divine.”

What not to say

Don’t claim the Greek simply means “declare” and move on. Don’t say Jesus only pretended not to know. And don’t split Christ into two persons, one divine and one human. The Christian claim is one Jesus, truly God and truly man.
