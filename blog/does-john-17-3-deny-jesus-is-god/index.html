Does John 17:3 Deny That Jesus Is God?

Jesus calls the Father “the only true God”, but the same prayer and the whole Gospel of John include the Son within God’s eternal identity.

Published 29 July 2026 · Updated 29 July 2026

Practise in the appBack to blog
Answer in brief

John 17:3 does not deny that Jesus is God. Jesus names the Father as the only true God while also placing Himself beside the Father as the source of eternal life and claiming the glory He shared with Him before creation. John presents Father and Son as distinct persons within the one divine being.

John 17:3 should be taken seriously, not explained away. Jesus prays that people may know the Father, “the only true God”, and Jesus Christ whom He sent. The verse distinguishes the Son from the Father. It does not turn the Son into a creature.

What is Jesus contrasting in John 17:3?

Jesus is contrasting the true God with idols and false gods. He is not setting the Father against the Son. The sentence joins knowing the Father and knowing Jesus Christ as the content of eternal life. No prophet or angel is placed beside God in that way.

The distinction matters. The Father is not the Son, and the Son is not the Father. Historic Christianity does not teach that Jesus is the Father wearing another mask. It teaches one God in three persons.

What does the rest of John 17 say about Jesus?

Two verses later, Jesus asks the Father to glorify Him “with the glory that I had with you before the world existed” (John 17:5). That is not the language of a created messenger. It points to the Son’s eternal fellowship and glory with the Father.

The wider Gospel says the same. John 1:1 says the Word was with God and was God. John 5:23 says all must honour the Son just as they honour the Father. Thomas calls the risen Jesus “My Lord and my God” in John 20:28, and Jesus accepts the confession.

How does Presbyterian theology read this verse?

The Westminster Confession of Faith 2.3 says that Father, Son and Holy Spirit are “of one substance, power, and eternity”. That reflects the pattern of John’s Gospel: real personal distinction without division of the one God.

John 17:3 therefore affirms the Father as the only true God without excluding the Son from deity. The Father is God, the Son is God, and there is only one God. The Son’s being sent describes His saving mission, not a lesser nature.

How to say it gently

You could say: “I agree that Jesus is speaking to the Father as the only true God. But the same prayer says Jesus shared the Father’s glory before creation. John distinguishes Father and Son while presenting both within the one divine identity. Could we read verses 3 and 5 together?”

What not to say

Don’t pretend the verse says something else. Don’t claim Jesus is secretly calling Himself the Father. And don’t answer one verse with a list of unrelated proof texts. Start with the prayer itself, then show how it fits John’s whole account.

Read John 17 as one prayer. Let the distinction between Father and Son stand, and let the Son’s eternal glory stand too.
