Why Does God Reveal Himself Through a Book?

Written Scripture gives Christians a public, stable and testable revelation instead of leaving us dependent on private impressions.

Published 19 August 2026 · Updated 19 August 2026

Practise in the appBack to blog
Answer in brief

God reveals Himself through Scripture because written words can preserve, clarify and publicly communicate truth across generations. The Bible gives Christians a stable standard that can be read, checked and taught, rather than leaving faith dependent on private impressions. Scripture does not replace knowing God; it anchors that relationship in what God has actually said.

Why would God use a book? Why not speak directly to every person whenever they need guidance? Christianity does not say God has revealed Himself only through writing. God has made Himself known through creation, prophets, mighty acts and supremely through Jesus Christ. But Scripture has a special role because it gives the church a public record of revelation that can be examined rather than merely claimed.

Why is written revelation useful?

Words on a page can travel across centuries without depending on one person’s memory. They can be copied, translated, compared and taught. That is part of the value of passages such as John 20:30–31, where John explains that signs were written so readers may believe. Writing preserves testimony for people who were not present when the events happened.

It also makes claims checkable. If someone says, “God told me,” another person may have no way to test the claim. Scripture gives Christians a common standard. 2 Timothy 3:14–17 presents the sacred writings as able to make believers wise for salvation and equip them for faithful living.

Does this mean God never guides Christians personally?

Christians disagree about how to describe impressions, promptings and extraordinary guidance today. A Reformed answer starts with a simpler point: no private impression carries the authority of Scripture. The Westminster Confession says the whole counsel of God necessary for His glory, salvation, faith and life is either expressly set down in Scripture or can be drawn from it by good and necessary consequence.

That does not mean God is absent from ordinary decisions. Christians pray for wisdom, seek counsel, use sanctified judgement and trust God’s providence. James 1:5 tells believers to ask God for wisdom. But wisdom is different from treating every strong feeling as a new revelation from God.

Why not rely mainly on an inner voice?

Because our inner life is fallible. We can mistake fear, desire, habit or confirmation bias for divine direction. The current debate around “hearing God’s voice” shows how easily Christians can become anxious about whether they have detected the right impression. Scripture gives us something firmer: words outside ourselves that can correct us rather than simply echo us.

This is one reason the approved Answerable question is so useful: without a stable written standard, people can confuse God’s voice with emotion, rumour, power or tradition. The point is not that God is trapped inside a book. The point is that God has graciously given His people a public norm by which every other claim can be tested.

Does a book make faith less personal?

No. A letter from someone you love is not less personal because it is written. Scripture is not a replacement for relationship with God; it is one of the chief means God uses to bring us to Christ and keep us anchored in truth. Luke 24:27 shows Jesus Himself opening the Scriptures to explain how they point to Him.

In my view, this is where the Christian answer is strongest. A purely private religion would be difficult to distinguish from self-talk. Christianity instead makes public claims about what God has done in history and gives those claims to us in texts that can be examined by believers and sceptics alike.

How to say it gently

You could say: “Christians don’t believe God is limited to a book. We believe He has acted in history and revealed Himself most clearly in Jesus. Scripture gives us a stable public record of that revelation, so our faith does not depend on whether we can correctly interpret a private feeling or impression.”

What not to say

Do not tell someone that every claimed experience of guidance is automatically fake. And do not imply that reading the Bible is merely collecting information. The stronger point is that Scripture has unique authority: experiences, impressions and advice must be tested rather than treated as equal to God’s written Word.
