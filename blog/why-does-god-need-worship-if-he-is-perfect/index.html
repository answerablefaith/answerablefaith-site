Why Does God Need Worship If He Is Perfect?

God doesn’t command worship because He lacks something; He calls us to worship because He alone is worthy and because we were made to know Him.

Published 10 August 2026 · Updated 10 August 2026

Practise in the app Back to blog
Answer in brief

God does not need worship to supply something missing in Him. He is complete, self-sufficient and worthy in Himself. He commands worship because truth requires that we honour the Creator above creation, and because our deepest good is found in knowing, loving and enjoying the God who made us.

Why does God need worship if He is perfect? The Christian answer starts by correcting one word: He doesn’t need it. Acts 17:24–25 says God is not served by human hands “as though he needed anything”. Worship does not fill an emotional gap in God.

Does God command worship because He is insecure?

No. Insecurity comes from lack: a person craves praise because he needs reassurance. Christianity says God has no such lack. He is the source of life, goodness and every created gift. Romans 11:36 says, “from him and through him and to him are all things”.

This is also why God’s call to worship is not vanity. If God is truly the highest good, it would be false for Him to point us to something lesser as though it deserved the highest place. Matthew 4:10 says, “You shall worship the Lord your God and him only shall you serve.”

Why does worship matter for us?

Worship tells the truth about reality. God is Creator; we are creatures. He is the giver; we are receivers. That doesn’t make human beings worthless. It gives us a secure place. We do not have to pretend to be self-made or self-sufficient.

Worship also directs our love. We always treat something as ultimate: success, approval, money, comfort, romance, politics or ourselves. Scripture calls those rivals idols because they cannot bear the weight we place on them. God’s command to worship Him is therefore protective as well as rightful.

Is God glorified at our expense?

No. God’s glory and our true good belong together. Psalm 16:11 says that in God’s presence there is “fullness of joy”. The Westminster Shorter Catechism captures this well: our chief end is “to glorify God, and to enjoy him for ever”. God does not gain glory because He was short of it. We come to see and delight in the glory that is already His.

The Westminster Confession of Faith 2.2 makes the same point: God has all life, glory, goodness and blessedness in Himself and does not depend on His creatures. That gives worship a very different tone from feeding a divine ego. It is the creature rejoicing in the Creator.

How to say it gently

You could say: “I don’t think God asks for worship because He needs compliments. Christianity says He needs nothing. If God is the source of all goodness, then worship is recognising what is true and learning to love the highest good rather than making created things ultimate.”

What not to say

Don’t answer as though God’s worth depends on our praise. Don’t suggest worship is a bargain where we flatter God so He will bless us. And don’t mock the objection. It is reasonable to ask why a perfect God commands worship; the answer is His worth and our good, not His insecurity.

A useful next step is to read Acts 17:24–25, Romans 11:36 and Psalm 16:11 together. Then see how this fits with why Christians believe God is uncreated and self-existent and why God can feel hidden even though He has made Himself known.
