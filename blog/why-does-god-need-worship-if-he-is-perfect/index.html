Doctrine of God · Worship and God’s self-sufficiency

Why Does God Need Worship If He Is Perfect?

God does not command worship because He lacks something; He calls us to worship because He alone is worthy and because we were made for Him.

Published 10 August 2026 · Updated 10 August 2026

Practise in the app Back to blog
Answer in brief

God does not need worship to supply something missing in Him. He is self-existent, complete and blessed in Himself. He commands worship because He alone is worthy, because creatures should honour their Creator, and because our deepest good is found in knowing, loving and enjoying the God who made us.

Why does God need worship if He is perfect? He does not. That matters because the objection often imagines God as a needy ruler demanding compliments. Scripture gives a very different picture. Acts 17:24–25 says God is not served by human hands “as though he needed anything”. Worship does not repair a lack in God.

Does God command worship because He is insecure?

No. Insecurity belongs to creatures who depend on approval. God depends on no one. Psalm 50:10–12 makes the point sharply: the world and all its fullness already belong to Him. He does not ask for worship because heaven is running short of praise.

We think this distinction changes the whole tone of the question. If God were one powerful being among others, demanding worship could look like vanity. But if He is the uncreated source of all being, goodness and truth, then worship is simply the fitting response to reality. Romans 11:36 says, “from him and through him and to him are all things”.

Why is God right to command worship?

Because God would be false to Himself if He treated anything else as more worthy than He is. Matthew 4:10 says, “You shall worship the Lord your God and him only shall you serve.” That command is not divine insecurity. It is moral truth: the Creator deserves what no creature deserves.

There is also a deeper point. God’s glory is not a reputation He builds from our applause. It is the splendour of who He eternally is. Our worship does not add one ounce to His worth. It acknowledges worth that was already there before any creature existed.

Our view is that this is where many modern objections go wrong. They assume praise works the same way for God as it does for us. It does not. Human praise can inflate ego. Worship of God rightly orders the creature because God is not competing with us for status.

Why does worship matter for us?

Worship tells the truth about who God is and who we are. He is Creator; we are creatures. He gives life; we receive it. That is humbling, but it is also freeing. We do not have to carry the impossible burden of being self-made, self-sufficient or self-defining.

Worship also directs our loves. Human beings do not live without an ultimate concern. If it is not God, it will be success, approval, money, comfort, sex, politics, family or ourselves. The Bible calls that idolatry because created things make terrible gods. They cannot carry the weight of our identity, hope and obedience.

This is why we would go further than saying worship is merely God’s right. It is also mercy. God turns us away from lesser masters that consume us and back to Himself, the only One who can bear the weight of our trust.

Is God glorified at our expense?

No. Scripture joins God’s glory to the good of His people rather than placing them in competition. Psalm 16:11 says that in God’s presence there is “fullness of joy”. John 17:3 describes eternal life as knowing the true God and Jesus Christ whom He sent.

That fits a classic Reformed insight without needing a denominational label: humanity’s highest end is to glorify God and enjoy Him. Those two ends are not rivals. The more clearly we see God’s worth, the more rightly ordered our joy becomes.

We think this is one of Christianity’s most beautiful claims about worship. God is not made richer by our praise, yet we are made richer by knowing Him. The command to worship therefore protects both truth and joy.

Does God need us for anything?

No. God’s self-sufficiency means He has life and fullness in Himself. He created freely, not because loneliness or need forced Him to. Exodus 3:14 points to God’s self-existence in His name, “I AM WHO I AM”. This is also why our article on why God does not need a maker matters here: the God of Christianity is not one dependent being in a chain of causes.

That does not make God cold or distant. His love is more secure because it is free. He does not love creatures because He needs something back from them. He gives because He is good.

What does this tell us about idolatry?

Idolatry is not only bowing before a statue. It is giving created things the trust, obedience or hope that belongs to God. The problem is not that God feels threatened by rivals. The problem is that false gods deform the people who trust them.

This connects with the question of why God can feel hidden. If we expect God to behave like a visible object or a needy person, we will misunderstand both His transcendence and His self-revelation. God calls us away from invented versions of Him and toward who He has actually revealed Himself to be.

How to say it gently

You could say: “Christians do not believe God needs compliments. He needs nothing. If God is the source of all life and goodness, then worship is recognising what is true and giving the highest love to the One who is actually worthy of it.”

What not to say

Do not make it sound as though God’s worth rises when people praise Him. Do not describe worship as a bargain where we flatter God so He will bless us. And do not dismiss the objection as silly. It is a fair question, but it depends on picturing God as needy rather than self-sufficient.

A useful next step is to read Acts 17:24–25, Romans 11:36, Psalm 16:11 and John 17:3 together. Then compare this with how God’s sovereignty relates to human responsibility and why God is uncreated and self-existent. The same doctrine of God runs through all three questions: He is not one more dependent being inside creation.
